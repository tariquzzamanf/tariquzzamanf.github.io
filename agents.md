# Agent instructions — academic website

## Working agreement

- Follow the user’s latest instructions. Keep durable design and workflow decisions here; do not duplicate biographies, publication records, metrics, or personal favorites.
- Use the PDF and LaTeX CV in `files/cv/` for factual records, together with approved sources in `content/` and explicit user corrections. Do not invent dates, interests, claims, or publication status. Preserve author order and distinguish accepted work from preprints. Never call an arXiv-only paper “accepted”, in news, badges, or copy: it is a preprint. `is_preprint()` in the builder suppresses the Accepted badge and fails the build if a news entry calls a preprint accepted.
- Maintain this guide in place. Do not create additional Markdown guides unless requested.
- Keep work inside this project. Verify the Git root before staging; never stage the parent home directory. Do not push or publish unless requested.

## Design philosophy

Create an original, reading-focused academic website. Help visitors identify the person, understand their work, find outputs, and make contact. Give Home, Research, Publications, CV, and Personal distinct purposes within consistent navigation.

- Use warm cream paper, muted green accents, subtle borders, polished buttons, and restrained motion. Use Bookerly when installed, otherwise bundled Literata; no external font service. The browser loads `assets/fonts/literata.woff2`, a Latin subset (Latin-1, Latin Extended-A, general punctuation, arrows) of the variable font with both the `opsz` and `wght` axes intact, so `font-weight:200 900` keeps working. `literata.ttf` and `literata-light.ttf` must stay: `files/cv/tariq.tex` compiles the CV from them, so never delete, subset, or re-encode those two. Literata's OFL names no Reserved Font Name, so the subset keeps the family name.
- Establish hierarchy through typography and spacing. Keep comfortable line lengths and make supporting metadata quieter than titles. Avoid generic slogans, decorative straplines, and repeated information.
- Keep substantive content and navigation usable without JavaScript. Use JavaScript only to enhance theme controls, mobile navigation, and active-section highlighting.
- Make layouts responsive, preserve semantic reading order, and support keyboard focus, readable contrast, and reduced motion.
- Keep documentation and site content self-contained, without references to an external design-inspiration website.

## Page and interaction rules

- **Home:** preserve the approved biography, research-interest cards, and personal section. Keep a single “Recent” section after the research-interest cards and before Personal: every `news.json` entry through `{{NEWS}}`, sorted newest first by its `Mon YYYY` date in the builder (file order does not matter), with the shared `.section-heading` pattern and an “All publications →” link. No expander, no cap, and no selected-publications block. Keep desktop biography text at 19px. The name `h1` is the largest element on Home (about 3.9rem, weight 650) with a short green-to-gold accent rule beneath it, so it leads over the coloured buttons. On mobile, use name heading → portrait/caption → biography in DOM order. Center the portrait at up to 200px wide with natural proportions, hide the repeated small name, and retain role, institution, and location below the photo.
- **Research interests (Home and Research must agree):** one shared set of four categories, in this order — Misinformation & harmful content, LLM evaluation & bias, Low-resource & Bangla NLP, Accessibility & sign language. The same names are the Home card titles, the Research `h2` headings, and the Research “On this page” labels; renaming or reordering one means doing all four — the Home biography's “My work spans…” sentence names the same four in the same order and wording, as do the Home meta description and the JSON-LD `knowsAbout` list. Anchors are `#misinformation`, `#evaluation`, `#bangla`, `#accessibility`, plus `#community` for the non-topical closing section. A paper may appear under two categories with a different framing and a different descriptive title, as the ACL 2026 and BLP papers do; do not repeat the same wording twice.
- **Home interest cards:** tags carry published venues only — `ACL 2026`, `BLP @ EMNLP 2023`, `CV4A11y @ ICCV 2025`. No arXiv or preprint tags, no dataset, model, or metric keywords.
- **Links to a paper page** read “Project page →” wherever they appear (Research, Publications); “Website” was ambiguous. Home’s Recent items link paper titles to their paper pages, which carry the external links.
- **Research:** explain themes through concrete papers and resource links; paper titles must be smaller than section headings. Avoid redundant subtitles.
- **Publications:** place large publication/citation/h-index/i10-index metrics directly below the title and lead, without a box, border, or surface fill. Use Google Scholar and Download CV actions. Group entries as Conference papers, Journal articles, Workshop & shared-task papers, and Preprints, with C/J/W/P numbering and area tags. Preserve paper anchors and Code & data. Calculate publication totals from metadata; show unavailable metrics as dashes, treating zero as a valid value.
- **Paper pages (`publications/<id>/`):** built from each paper's `page` object in `publications.json`. The header is breadcrumb (Publications / group) → type · year → title → venue, award, tags → Paper, arXiv, PDF, Code, Dataset, and Video buttons; no author list or affiliations, since the BibTeX and the paper carry them. Below it, the shared reading layout: an “On this page” nav with short `nav` labels (a single swipeable sticky row on phones, keeping the active link in view), then overview (teaser figure plus “In short” callout with the full abstract collapsed), paper-specific sections, numbered `<details>` findings, video, “Cite this paper” with only the BibTeX and copy button, acknowledgements when the paper has them, and previous/next paper links in Publications-page order. The main nav marks Publications as current. Everything on these pages comes from the paper itself; do not add claims or numbers it does not state. No self-linking “Website” button. Figures are cropped from the paper PDFs into `files/papers/<id>/` as WebP, each with a visible “View full size” caption link; tables are HTML, not images, and scroll inside their own box with edge shadows.
- **CV:** retain approved appointments without restoring removed entries. Teaching is grouped as the PDF groups it: a Junior Lecturer appointment record, then a `Courses` group and a `Supervision` group, each a `.course-list`. Membership is derived from `teaching.json` by the `(Supervisor)`/`(Co-Supervisor)` marker, so roles belong in the data, not the template. Semesters are not shown on either the PDF or the page. Use full course names from the approved content.
- **CV ordering (PDF and page):** research precedes teaching, so the record reads as a researcher who teaches. The PDF runs Research Interests → Education → Research → Research & Professional Experience → Teaching & Supervision → Awards; the page runs Education → Research & professional → Teaching & Supervision → Awards, mirroring the PDF; the Junior Lecturer appointment sits inside Teaching & Supervision as it does there, with `#appointments` kept as an empty `.legacy-anchor` span so older links still land. Keep the two consistent.
- **CV restraint (graduate applications):** keep teaching to a grouped course line and a supervision line; do not add per-course detail, Skills, Service, or Talks sections, and do not print citation counts or h-index. No separate Datasets & Software section either — it repeats the papers, so each artifact rides on its publication entry as [Code] or [Data]. The RedDot entry reads “Machine Learning Intern” with no task detail, on the PDF and the page alike. Undergraduate supervision stays under Teaching & Supervision, worded as undergraduate, not under research. Secondary-school records (SSC) and the school-level Best Student Award were removed deliberately; do not restore them. Label the preprint P to match the site, and claim no submission venue for it. The PDF carries no "Last updated" dateline; it reads as unprofessional on application material.
- **CV actions:** Home, Publications, and CV buttons read “Download CV” (omit “Full” and “PDF”) with the PDF icon and the external-link mark, and open `files/cv/tariq.pdf` in a new tab (`target="_blank" rel="noopener"`) — no `download` attribute.
- **Buttons and icons:** no button is singled out as primary. Every `.btn` is a solid fill in the colour of its `data-kind` (mail, project, paper, arxiv, pdf, code, dataset, video, scholar, github, cv, copy) with light text; keep each fill at 4.5:1 or better against that text. “See more” routes (section-heading links, Read more) stay plain text links with a sliding arrow. Icons are inline SVG from `ICONS` in `scripts/build.py`: build code calls `icon()`/`resource_button()`, and content files write `{{icon:name}}`, which `render()` expands. External links carry a small trailing ↗ icon, internal routes a trailing →. Do not go back to text glyphs or CSS `::before` markers.
- **Section headings:** section `h2`s (Home `.section-heading`, CV and Research chapters, Publications groups and Code & data, Personal panels, paper-page sections) are tinted bands with a coloured left rule, pinned under the sticky header while their section is on screen; on phones they pin below the “On this page” row via `--contents-height` and shrink to one compact line. Colours cycle through six `--sec-*` tokens by section order (Research themes keep fixed colours), with lighter dark-mode variants.
- **Section navigation:** Research, Publications, CV, and Personal share “On this page” navigation. Keep every link visible below the sticky header and highlight the active section while scrolling. On phones every such menu is one swipeable sticky row (no eyebrow, 44px targets, edge shadows on the side with more links) that scrolls itself to keep the active link in view — never a multi-row grid, which eats the screen. Preserve stable anchors and correct scroll offsets.
- **Personal:** order topics Anime → Movies → Books → Sports, matching homepage links. Preserve the approved introduction and owner-supplied rankings. Describe collections as all-time favorites, not complete viewing histories; derive counts from supplied entries rather than inventing picks.
- **Favorites grid:** five columns on desktop (over 1000px), three on tablets, two at 800px and below.
- **Favorite cards:** each whole card, including image, title, author, and padding, is one Wikipedia link. No repeated provider labels or nested links. Use only a soft drop shadow and subtle 1.015-scale zoom on hover, without borders, background changes, or upward lift. Preserve visible keyboard focus and disable motion for reduced-motion preferences.
- **Artwork:** load covers remotely; do not store artwork binaries in the repository. Keep provenance in metadata and the shared provider/rights-holder note. Remote hosting does not imply a copyright license.
