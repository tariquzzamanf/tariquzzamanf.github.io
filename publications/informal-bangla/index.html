An informal Bangla FastText embedding, trained on 3.8 million social-media comments, lets a lightweight BiLSTM detect violence-inciting text within about four macro-F1 points of BanglaBERT while training 17 times faster. The paper received the Best Shared Task Paper Award at BLP 2023.

Full abstract

This paper introduces a novel informal Bangla word embedding for designing a cost-efficient solution for the task “Violence Inciting Text Detection,” which focuses on developing classification systems to categorize violence that can potentially incite further violent actions. We propose a semi-supervised learning approach by training an informal Bangla FastText embedding, which is further fine-tuned on lightweight models on the task-specific dataset and yielded competitive results to our initial method using BanglaBERT, which secured the 7th position with an F1-score of 73.98%. We conduct extensive experiments to assess the efficiency of the proposed embedding and how well it generalizes in terms of violence classification, along with its coverage on the task’s dataset. Our proposed Bangla IFT embedding achieved a competitive macro average F1 score of 70.45%. Additionally, we provide a detailed analysis of our findings, delving into potential causes of misclassification in the detection of violence-inciting text.

Violence-inciting text detection

BLP-2023 Task 1 asks systems to sort text by the violence it could incite. Unlike hate speech, which targets groups by attribute, violence-inciting text advocates harm. The shared-task data are YouTube comments on the nine most significant violent incidents in the Bengal region, covering Bangladesh and West Bengal, over the past decade.

  • Direct violence: explicit threats against individuals or communities.
  • Passive violence: derogatory language, abusive remarks, slang, or justifications for violence.
  • Non-violence: content unrelated to violence, including discussion of social rights.

Our initial BanglaBERT submission placed 7th with an F1 of 73.98%. This paper asks whether a far cheaper system can come close.

An informal Bangla embedding

We collected 6.8 million comments from Facebook and YouTube through the Facebook Graph API using Facepager. Removing redundant words, symbols, and non-Bangla content left 3.8 million samples. A FastText model was trained on them with continuous bag-of-words, 300-dimensional vectors, character n-grams of 3 to 6, and a minimum word count of 2. Sub-word information lets it represent misspelled and out-of-vocabulary words common in informal text.

The resulting informal FastText (IFT) embedding covers far more of the task vocabulary than BanglaBERT’s.

IFT is paired with logistic regression, SVM, LSTM, BiLSTM, and GRU classifiers, each also trained with a plain CBoW embedding for comparison, and set against mBERT, XLM-RoBERTa, and BanglaBERT.

Findings

01The informal embedding lifts every lightweight model.

Replacing a plain CBoW embedding with IFT improves macro-F1 for all five lightweight classifiers, most dramatically for BiLSTM and GRU, which with CBoW barely predict the violent classes at all.

02BiLSTM with IFT beats mBERT and XLM-RoBERTa.

At 70.5% macro-F1, BiLSTM with IFT outperforms mBERT (65.8%) and XLM-RoBERTa (67.4%). BanglaBERT remains the strongest model at 74.6%, underlining the value of language-specific pre-training.

03It trains 17 times faster and infers 1.54 times faster than BanglaBERT.

Measured on a Google Colab T4 GPU, with six training epochs over 2,700 samples and inference over 2,016 samples.

Across models, most errors come from telling passive from direct violence, likely because violent content shares so much wording. IFT still improves results on these harder classes.
