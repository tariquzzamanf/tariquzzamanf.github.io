What I work on

Research

I study how language technologies behave when the language, context, and people they serve are not treated as edge cases. My work connects evaluation with practical resources for harmful-content detection, Bangla NLP, and sign-language access.

01 · Understanding harm in context

Misinformation & harmful content

I study how harmful and misleading language can be identified in the conditions where it actually circulates: across languages, inside realistic social contexts, and in informal or code-mixed writing. The goal is to make detection systems more dependable when the evidence is uneven and the consequences of error are serious.

I am especially interested in the gap between benchmark performance and real-world judgment. A system should not change its assessment simply because a claim is framed for a different community, region, or social role. This means evaluating not only whether a model is accurate, but also whether its reasoning remains stable when context changes.

02 · Testing the model behind the answer

LLM evaluation & bias

My evaluation work asks what large language models are responding to when they make a judgment. I examine how language, identity cues, roles, regions, and surrounding scenarios shape model behaviour, particularly in tasks where an apparently small shift can change the outcome.

Rather than treating a single score as a complete account of quality, I design evaluations that expose variation: which groups are affected, in which direction, and under what conditions. This helps turn vague concerns about bias into evidence that can guide model development and responsible use.

03 · Building for the language people use

Low-resource & Bangla NLP

Bangla has a large speaker community but comparatively little task-specific language technology. I work on ways to build useful NLP systems when labelled data is small, spelling is variable, and online writing freely mixes Bangla with English and other forms of informal expression.

This domain brings together representation learning, data-efficient training, augmentation, and careful evaluation. The broader aim is to make low-resource research answer practical questions: what data is missing, whose language is being overlooked, and how can a method remain useful outside a carefully cleaned dataset?

04 · Making language technology usable

Accessibility & sign language

I explore language technology for people who are often left out of mainstream NLP design, with a focus on Bangla Sign Language and the lack of accessible instructional material around it.

My interest is both technical and practical: how can a system represent the parameters that distinguish a sign, generate guidance that learners can understand, and support resources that communities can build on? Accessibility here is a design constraint from the beginning, not a final layer added after a model is trained.
